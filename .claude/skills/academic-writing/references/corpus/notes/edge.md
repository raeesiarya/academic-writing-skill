# Style notes: edge / accelerator / TinyML corpus

Papers: EIE (ISCA 2016), TPU (ISCA 2017), Integer-only quantization "IntQuant" (CVPR 2018), MCUNet (NeurIPS 2020), TFLM (MLSys 2021), Eyeriss v2 (JETCAS 2019).
These notes cover how the papers are written, not what they found. Every quote was checked against the source text: `txt/`, plus a reading-order re-extraction from the same PDFs for two-column papers, where `txt/` interleaves the columns.

## 1. Per-paper notes

### EIE: a factor-by-factor energy argument
- **Abstract** (~200 words): problem, then the physical cause (DRAM vs ALU energy), the prior enabler (Deep Compression), and the proposal. It then breaks the savings into factors: "Going from DRAM to SRAM gives EIE 120× energy saving; Exploiting sparsity saves 10×; Weight sharing gives 8×". It ends with x-factors against CPU, GPU and DaDianNao. The abstract holds about eleven numbers, and each answers a different question.
- **Intro gap** comes as a back-of-envelope calculation built from a cited 45nm energy table: "Running a 1G connection neural network, for example, at 20Hz would require (20Hz)(1G)(640pJ) = 12.8W just for DRAM accesses". The spec preview gives node and clock: "In 45nm CMOS technology, an EIE PE has an area of 0.638mm2 and dissipates 9.16mW at 800MHz." The numbered contributions claim priority ("We present the first accelerator for sparse and weight sharing neural networks."), and the roadmap is a plain paragraph ("In Section II we describe the motivation of accelerating compressed networks.").
- **Architecture** walks through a toy 16×8 matrix on 4 PEs, then one bold run-in heading per hardware unit ("Activation Queue and Load Balancing."). Bit widths are given inline: "EIE pointers are 16-bits in length".
- **Design-space exploration** ends each sweep with a decision: "Thus, we choose 8 as the optimal queue depth." Tradeoffs are spelled out in words: wider SRAM interfaces "reduce the number of total SRAM accesses, but increase the energy cost per SRAM read."
- **Methodology** names the simulator, the RTL and synthesis flow ("under the TSMC 45nm GP standard VT library with worst case PVT corner"), and the exact baseline libraries ("cuSPARSE CSRMV"). Indirect power measurements state their assumptions ("assumed 15% AC to DC conversion loss").
- **Normalization honesty:** "Our actual savings are about 10× less than this number because of index overhead and because EIE is implemented in 45nm technology compared to the 28nm technology used by the Titan-X GPU". The paper also adds a projected column ("The right column projected EIE to the same technology (28nm) as the other platforms") and justifies batch 1 ("Since assembling a batch adds significant amounts of latency, we consider the case when batch size = 1").
- **Negative result reported:** "With 8-bits fixed-point, however, the accuracy dropped to only 53%, which becomes intolerable." Related work comes late. The Discussion maps the design space and flags the authors' own choice: "A second approach (ours) is to distribute matrix rows to PEs."

### TPU: a measurement paper with a confident, conversational voice
- **Abstract** (~240 words) opens with a field belief: "Many architects believe that major improvements in cost-energy-performance must now come from domain-specific hardware." It continues with the artifact's specs, a *why* sentence (deterministic execution suits 99th-percentile latency), baselines framed as fair ("contemporaries deployed in the same datacenters"), and results given as ranges ("about 15X - 30X faster"). A weakness is conceded up front: "Despite low utilization for some applications".
- **Intro** ends with "Here is a preview of the highlights:", a list of one-number bullets. One bullet challenges the community: "While most architects have been accelerating CNNs, they represent just 5% of our datacenter workload."
- **Design rationale** is told as history plus constraint: "Sparse architectural support was omitted for time-to-deploy reasons." Sizes are derived: "We picked 4096 by first noting that the operations per byte need to reach peak performance (roofline knee in Section 4) is ~1350".
- **Normalization** is explicit: "we usually show results below normalized per die (Figures 5-8, Figures 10-11, and Tables 3, 4, and 6), but we occasionally show whole systems (Figure 9)." Baseline fairness is argued: "they also had to be sensibly configured machines, and not awkward artifacts assembled solely to win benchmarks." Mean choice is justified: "Recall that architects use the geometric mean when they don’t know the actual mix of programs that will be run".
- **Models are caveated and validated:** "This simple visual model is not perfect, yet it offers insights on the causes of performance bottlenecks." The performance model is checked against hardware counters, with differences that "average below 10%". Anomalies are diagnosed rather than hidden ("Table 3 explains what happened with CNN1"), with cell-level pointers ("(column 7, row 1)").
- **Limitations** are short and parenthetical: "For business reasons, we can’t publish such price information"; "(The short design schedule prevented inclusion of many energy-saving features.)". What-if estimates are bracketed on both sides: "This change is both optimistic, since it doesn’t include CPU time to run its share of the app, and pessimistic".
- **Discussion** uses "the fallacy and pitfall with rebuttal style of [Hen18]". Each item is a bold misconception followed by measured data, which pre-empts baseline objections ("Fallacy: The K80 GPU results would be much better if Boost mode were enabled.").
- **Related work** (late) is historical, then contemporary, and it addresses contradictions head-on: "The Fathom benchmark paper seemingly reports results contradictory to ours". Cross-domain comparisons are flagged: "Although it’s apples versus oranges".
- **Conclusion** is a set of lessons ("We found that", "We observed that", "We also learned that") plus a coined maxim: "low utilization of a huge, cheap resource can still deliver high, cost-effective performance."
- **Avoid:** exclamation marks ("TPU IPS vary by 75X!") and cheerleading ("soars to an amazing 69X - 196X").

### IntQuant: a method paper built on two critiques
- **Abstract** (~120 words) contains no numbers and states the claim as a Pareto shift: "improves the tradeoff between accuracy and on-device latency".
- **Intro gap** is two numbered criticisms: "First, prior approaches have not been evaluated on a reasonable baseline architecture." / "Second, many quantization approaches do not deliver verifiable efficiency improvements on real hardware." The sharpest line: "Notably, these approaches rarely provide on-device measurements to verify the promised timing improvements." Bulleted contributions each carry a section pointer.
- **Method** derives step by step and gives each constraint a hardware reason: "we eschew implementations requiring lookup tables because these tend to perform poorly compared to pure arithmetic on SIMD hardware". It justifies a notation choice ("We use C++ syntax because it allows the unambiguous conveyance of types.") and marks empirical facts as empirical ("We empirically find it to always be in the interval (0, 1)"). Types are exact throughout (uint8, int32). Footnotes point to the shipping code.
- **Experiments** name the cores and setup ("Latency was measured on a single thread using Snapdragon 835 cores (big and LITTLE)."). Losses are signed and given a basis ("a minimal loss in accuracy (−1.8% relative)"). The paper argues for its own metric ("we advocate for a more comprehensive latency-vs-accuracy tradeoff as a better measure") and says where gains shrink ("Note that this tradeoff depends critically on the relative speed of floating-point vs integer-only arithmetic in hardware.").
- **Ablation** findings come as an inline numbered list: "The tables suggest that 1) weights are more sensitive to reduced quantization bit depth than activations". The close is hedged: "suggests that integer-arithmetic-only inference could be a key enabler".

### MCUNet: a systems-plus-ML paper driven by a hard constraint
- **Abstract**: "appealing but challenging: the memory of microcontrollers is 2-3 orders of magnitude smaller even than mobile phones". It then gives x-factors against named libraries ("compared to TF-Lite Micro [3] and CMSIS-NN [28]") and a scoped priority claim ("on an off-the-shelf commercial microcontroller").
- **Intro** pins the constraint to a part: "As shown in Table 1, a state-of-the-art ARM Cortex-M7 MCU only has 320kB SRAM and 1MB Flash storage". Overruns are given as multiples ("ResNet-50 [21] exceeds the storage limit by 100×"), and the two budgets are separated: "SRAM constrains the activation size (read&write); Flash constrains the model size (read-only)."
- **Method** uses run-in headings. Each one names the current practice, says why it fails on MCUs, gives the replacement, and states the measured gain ("reduces the measured memory usage by 1.6× as shown in Figure 5"). Heuristics are labeled and then verified: "The intuition is that, within the same model family, the accuracy is usually positively related to the computation" and "We further verify the the assumption in Section 4.5."
- **Setup** lists boards with core and SRAM/Flash and sets a default ("By default, we use STM32F746 to report the results unless otherwise specified."). Exclusions are justified: "We did not use MobileNetV3 [24]-alike models because the hard-swish activation is not efficiently supported on microcontrollers."
- **Results** use claim-first headings ("Co-design brings better performance.", "Search space optimization matters."). Hypotheses are labeled: "We hypothesize that using a super large space increases the difficulty of training super network and evolution search."
- **Avoid:** typos ("first to achieves", "the the"), salesy adverbs ("TinyEngine smartly adapts"), and slogans stated as findings ("the era of tiny machine learning on IoT devices has arrived").

### TFLM: a design-principles systems paper
- **Abstract** (~90 words) has no numbers ("we explain the design decisions behind TFLM"). This suits an infrastructure paper, not a results paper.
- **Intro**: a field definition, then the gap as a bullet list ("We can distill the major issues facing the frameworks into the following:"), then ordinal contributions ("First, we clearly lay out the challenges"). One of them pushes against received wisdom: an interpreter, "which is traditionally viewed as a low-performance alternative to compilation, is in fact highly suitable for the embedded domain".
- **Structure:** Challenges (§2), then Principles (§3, which cite §2), then Implementation, then Evaluation. This gives a traceable chain from principle to code. Principles are normative: "The design should exclude any other function, no matter how useful." They also get names ("We call it the “Bag of Files” principle.") and one analogy ("is like working with the elaborate CISC ISA without access to the ISA manual.").
- **Drawbacks are stated in the design section:** "This simplistic approach works well for initial prototyping, but it wastes memory"; "it does have some drawbacks."; "This strategy makes the process difficult to debug, maintain, and extend." An open problem is admitted: "is an unsolved problem".
- **Evaluation** justifies its scope ("So, we focus on extreme endpoints rather than on the overall spectrum."), discloses a compromise ("Because it is proprietary, we use a version with scrambled weights and biases."), and explains results causally ("only 25% better than the baseline reference model because less time goes to the kernel calculations"). Weakness: the evaluation is thin, with no energy results.

### Eyeriss v2: long-form architecture built on root-cause analysis
- **Abstract** gives one fully qualified headline: "Eyeriss v2 in a 65nm CMOS process achieves a throughput of 1470.6 inferences/sec and 2560.3 inferences/J at a batch size of 1". Node, metric, batch and baseline appear in one sentence.
- **Intro** builds the gap from a defined quantity, data reuse ("the number of MACs that use the same piece of data, i.e., MACs/data"). It then splits into named failures: "more challenging in two ways." These become numbered run-in headings ("1) Array Utilization:"). Positioning is narrow: "In this work, we focus on complementary approaches that have been less explored". Contribution bullets each give an x-factor, a section pointer, and the normalization basis: "(scaled to the same number of PEs and storage capacity as Eyeriss v2)".
- **Architecture** goes top-level, then per data type, then per NoC mode, then per layer type, then down to circuits. A "Motivation" subsection precedes the mechanism. Alternatives are rejected on scaling cost ("the cost of its design increases quadratically with the number of nodes"), and simplifications are announced ("For simplicity, we are only showing a simplified case with 2 PE clusters with 2 PEs in each cluster"). Precision choices come with evidence: "Psums are accumulated at 20-bit precision, which has shown no accuracy impact in our experiments."
- **Ablation by variant** (v1/v1.5/v2): "In short, the comparison between v1 and v1.5 shows the impact of the hierarchical mesh network". Losses are reported: "However, the speedup of Eyeriss v2 over v1.5 is a mixed bag."
- **Methodology** is precise: "results reported in this section are from post-layout cycle-accurate gate-level simulations". Batch 1 is justified as "a more challenging setup for energy efficiency and throughput, but captures the low latency use case." Metrics are defined in footnotes.
- **Hedges are graded:** idealized assumptions are flagged ("we will assume no limitation on the off-chip bandwidth and no workload imbalance"), inferences about others are marked ("our understanding of those designs leads us to believe"), conjectures are labeled ("we conjecture that the NoC limitations can be addressed"), and gaps are admitted ("unfortunately, the available results for the other works are only on AlexNet.").
- **Avoid:** "This result proves that it is possible to build in high flexibility at a low cost." That is "proves" from one design point. Also avoid the excessive port-by-port figure narration.

## 2. Cross-cutting observations
- **Titles** take the form *Name: what it is*, or a descriptive scope phrase ("In-Datacenter", "on IoT Devices"). None uses a question or a pun.
- **Numbers:** hardware abstracts use x-factors over named baselines plus one or two absolute values with units. Method and infrastructure abstracts (IntQuant, TFLM) use none. Ranges are used when results vary by workload. The memory type is always named (SRAM, Flash or DRAM).
- **Normalization:** each hardware comparison states node, clock, batch, per-die vs per-system, scaling basis, and the power method (TDP vs measured, total vs incremental). Where the authors' own setup is at a disadvantage, they say so.
- **Figure references** carry the claim ("Table 3 explains what happened with CNN1"). TPU and MCUNet captions state the takeaway, not just the axes.
- **Related work** usually comes late (EIE, TPU, TFLM) or is folded into the gap analysis (IntQuant, Eyeriss v2). Prior work is positioned by capability ("Cnvlutin [39] only supports skipping cycles for activations and does not compress the weights").
- **Voice:** active "we" throughout. Past tense for what was done, present tense for what the system does. Typical sentences run 15–30 words, and topic sentences carry the claim.

## 3. Cross-paper patterns: transferable rules
1. **Motivate with a physical back-of-envelope calculation.** "Running a 1G connection neural network, for example, at 20Hz would require (20Hz)(1G)(640pJ) = 12.8W just for DRAM accesses" (EIE).
2. **Pin the constraint to a named part, then state the overrun as a multiple.** "a state-of-the-art ARM Cortex-M7 MCU only has 320kB SRAM and 1MB Flash storage" and "ResNet-50 [21] exceeds the storage limit by 100×" (MCUNet).
3. **Fully qualify every headline number.** "Eyeriss v2 in a 65nm CMOS process achieves a throughput of 1470.6 inferences/sec and 2560.3 inferences/J at a batch size of 1" (Eyeriss v2).
4. **Decompose gains into factors, and explain any shortfall from theory.** "Multiplying those factors 120×10×8×3 gives 28, 800× theoretical energy saving. Our actual savings are about 10× less than this number" (EIE).
5. **Declare the normalization basis, and flag where it changes.** "we usually show results below normalized per die ... but we occasionally show whole systems (Figure 9)" (TPU). Also "(scaled to the same number of PEs and storage capacity as Eyeriss v2)".
6. **Justify the benchmark setup and argue that the baselines are fair.** "Since assembling a batch adds significant amounts of latency, we consider the case when batch size = 1" (EIE). Also "not awkward artifacts assembled solely to win benchmarks" (TPU).
7. **Give every parameter a reason or a sweep, and end the sweep with a decision.** "Thus, we choose 8 as the optimal queue depth." (EIE). Also "We picked 4096 by first noting that..." (TPU).
8. **Write the gap as numbered, specific shortcomings of prior work.** "First, prior approaches have not been evaluated on a reasonable baseline architecture." (IntQuant).
9. **Make each ablation variant isolate one change, and name that change.** "In short, the comparison between v1 and v1.5 shows the impact of the hierarchical mesh network" (Eyeriss v2).
10. **Report losses and their causes.** "the deeper pipeline of the sparse PE actually makes the throughput slightly worse in the DW CONV layers." (Eyeriss v2). Also "With 8-bits fixed-point, however, the accuracy dropped to only 53%" (EIE).
11. **Grade hedges to the evidence.** Measured results get flat statements. Intuitions are labeled and then verified ("The intuition is that..." in MCUNet). Claims about others' hardware are labeled as conjecture ("we conjecture that the NoC limitations can be addressed", Eyeriss v2).
12. **State the drawbacks of your own design choices inside the design section.** "This simplistic approach works well for initial prototyping, but it wastes memory" (TFLM). Also "Sparse architectural support was omitted for time-to-deploy reasons." (TPU).
13. **Use bold run-in headings that state a claim or name a unit.** "Co-design brings better performance." (MCUNet). Also "Activation Queue and Load Balancing." (EIE).
14. **Pre-empt objections with fallacy/pitfall rebuttals backed by data.** "Fallacy: The K80 GPU results would be much better if Boost mode were enabled." It is followed by a measured 1.1X net gain (TPU).
15. **Define or defend the metric when the default misleads.** "Pitfall: For NN hardware, Inferences Per Second (IPS) is an inaccurate summary performance metric." (TPU). Also "we calculate GOPS based on the nominal number of operations in the DNN, i.e., including operations with data values of zero." (Eyeriss v2).

**Do not copy:** exclamation marks and hype ("soars to an amazing 69X - 196X"; "TinyEngine smartly adapts"); slogans presented as findings; "proves" drawn from one result; unscoped "first" claims (scope them as MCUNet does: "To the best of our knowledge"); typos; broader-impact sections that stretch past the evidence; and numberless abstracts for papers whose contribution is a measured result.
